# Judicial Data Governance Framework (JDGF)

> A scientific, methodological, and operational framework for governing judicial data, designed primarily for African judicial institutions and adaptable to other contexts.

## Purpose

The **Judicial Data Governance Framework (JDGF)** helps judicial institutions organize, govern, assess, and continuously improve the data they create, receive, maintain, exchange, and use.

JDGF addresses institutional responsibilities, policies, processes, controls, capabilities, evidence, maturity, assessment, implementation, and responsible artificial-intelligence readiness. It is intended to strengthen data quality, rights safeguards, judicial statistics, accountability, digital transformation, and human-centred justice.

JDGF is **not** a court-management system, database, analytics platform, predictive-justice solution, machine-learning model, certification scheme, or commercial product.

## Philosophy

**Human → Institution → Governance → Data → Digital → AI**

Technology is not the objective. Data governance must serve justice, institutional mandates, fundamental rights, public value, and the people who use or depend on judicial institutions. AI readiness does not require AI deployment.

## Programme positioning

JDGF is developed within the **Institutional Governance Research Program (IGRP)** under the **Programme Magnifica Humanitas**. The repository contains the authoritative JDGF framework documentation and separates it from supporting research and publication work.

## Canonical framework dimensions

JDGF v1.0 Candidate adopts eight interdependent dimensions:

1. Leadership and institutional governance;
2. Legal, ethical, and rights-based governance;
3. Data architecture and interoperability;
4. Data quality and metadata;
5. Security, privacy, and continuity;
6. Data lifecycle and records governance;
7. Use, statistics, transparency, and responsible innovation;
8. Artificial intelligence readiness.

The first seven dimensions provide the general judicial-data-governance architecture. The eighth is distinct because AI readiness is central to the JDGF theory of change and requires explicit human, institutional, legal, data, technical, and assurance preconditions.

## Maturity model

JDGF uses a formative capability model with scores from 0 to 5:

| Level | Name | Summary |
|---:|---|---|
| 0 | Absent | No established capability or credible evidence |
| 1 | Initial | Fragmented, informal, person-dependent, and reactive practices |
| 2 | Emerging | Partly defined or implemented practices with material gaps |
| 3 | Defined | Approved, assigned, repeatable, and substantially implemented capabilities |
| 4 | Managed | Measured, tested, reviewed, and demonstrably effective capabilities |
| 5 | Adaptive | Sustained, evidence-led, and continuously improved capabilities |

The model uses six recurring capability attributes, critical-control gates, confidence ratings, evidence requirements, and non-compensatory score caps. Higher maturity does not mean greater automation or mandatory AI adoption.

## Assessment scope

The documentary **JDGF Assessment Instrument** is part of v1.0 because it is required to apply the maturity model consistently. It remains a pilot instrument pending empirical validation.

A future **JDGAT** software product, hosted platform, automated scoring system, or certification tool remains outside the v1.0 scope. See [`docs/JDGF_V1_SCOPE_EXCLUSIONS.md`](docs/JDGF_V1_SCOPE_EXCLUSIONS.md).

## Language

English is the canonical normative language of the public v1.0 candidate repository. French may be used for working discussions, stakeholder engagement, explanatory summaries, and controlled translations. See [`docs/LANGUAGE_POLICY.md`](docs/LANGUAGE_POLICY.md).

## Current status

The repository is a **JDGF v1.0 Candidate — pre-empirical validation**. Its architecture and scoring methodology are specified, but no stable-release claim should be made until the validation programme is completed.

Major remaining requirements include:

- complete the clause-level standards mapping and canonical bibliography linkage;
- conduct structured multidisciplinary expert review;
- perform cognitive testing of the assessment instrument;
- complete at least one authorized institutional pilot;
- test inter-rater agreement, feasibility, fairness, and sensitivity to change;
- re-score and reconcile illustrative cases under the eight-dimension model;
- revise the framework and instrument from validation evidence;
- select the repository license and establish release governance.

See [`AUDIT.md`](AUDIT.md) and [`research/JDGF-Validation-Plan.md`](research/JDGF-Validation-Plan.md) for the readiness and validation plans.
