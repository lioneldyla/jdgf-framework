# Judicial Data Governance Framework (JDGF)

> A scientific, methodological, and operational framework for governing judicial data, designed primarily for African judicial institutions and adaptable to other contexts.

## Purpose

The **Judicial Data Governance Framework (JDGF)** helps judicial institutions organize, govern, assess, and continuously improve the data they create, receive, maintain, exchange, and use.

JDGF addresses institutional responsibilities, policies, processes, controls, capabilities, evidence, maturity, assessment, and implementation. It is intended to strengthen data quality, rights safeguards, judicial statistics, accountability, digital transformation, and responsible AI readiness.

JDGF is **not** a court-management system, database, analytics platform, predictive-justice solution, machine-learning model, or commercial product.

## Philosophy

**Human → Institution → Governance → Data → Digital → AI**

Technology is not the objective. Data governance must serve justice, institutional mandates, fundamental rights, public value, and the people who use or depend on judicial institutions.

## Programme positioning

JDGF is developed within the **Institutional Governance Research Program (IGRP)** under the **Programme Magnifica Humanitas**. The repository contains the authoritative JDGF framework documentation and separates it from supporting research and publication work.

## Proposed framework dimensions

The current working architecture includes:

1. Strategy and institutional alignment;
2. Leadership, roles, and accountability;
3. Legal, ethical, and rights safeguards;
4. Data architecture, metadata, and interoperability;
5. Data quality and records lifecycle;
6. Access, security, and resilience;
7. Judicial statistics, use, and decision support;
8. Capabilities, culture, and change;
9. Risk, assurance, and performance;
10. Digital transformation and responsible AI readiness.

These dimensions remain subject to literature review, practitioner consultation, institutional testing, and scientific validation before JDGF v1.0.

## Maturity model

JDGF uses five working maturity levels:

| Level | Name | Summary |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ad hoc | Fragmented, informal, and reactive practices |
| 2 | Defined | Initial roles, policies, and repeatable procedures |
| 3 | Managed | Operational controls, evidence, indicators, and monitoring |
| 4 | Integrated | Coordinated governance across functions and systems |
| 5 | Adaptive | Embedded, resilient, outcome-oriented, and continuously improved governance |

Maturity is assessed by dimension and must be supported by evidence. Higher maturity does not mean greater automation or mandatory AI adoption.

## Current status

The repository has been restructured as a **working draft toward JDGF v1.0**. The architecture and baseline documentation exist, but v1.0 is not yet scientifically or institutionally validated.

Major remaining requirements include:

- literature and standards mapping;
- validation of dimensions and terminology;
- detailed roles, controls, indicators, and maturity criteria;
- assessment toolkit development and testing;
- at least one authorized pilot or case study;
- independent scientific and practitioner review;
- licensing and release governance.

See [`AUDIT.md`](AUDIT.md) for the complete readiness checklist.
