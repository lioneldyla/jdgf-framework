# JDGF Foundations

## Status

**Working Draft — target: JDGF v1.0**

## Purpose

The Judicial Data Governance Framework (JDGF) provides judicial institutions with a coherent method for organizing, governing, evaluating, and improving judicial data.

## Problem statement

Judicial institutions produce essential operational, administrative, statistical, and case-related data. These data are often fragmented across systems and services, inconsistently documented, uneven in quality, and governed through unclear responsibilities. This weakens institutional steering, accountability, service delivery, reform evaluation, interoperability, and responsible digital transformation.

## Vision

Judicial institutions govern data as a strategic public resource in ways that strengthen justice, institutional accountability, evidence-based administration, fundamental rights, and public trust.

## General objective

Develop a reusable and context-sensitive framework that enables judicial institutions to establish, assess, and continuously improve data-governance capabilities.

## Specific objectives

JDGF should enable institutions to:

- clarify data ownership, stewardship, accountability, and decision rights;
- improve data quality, documentation, security, protection, and availability;
- align data practices with judicial mandates and applicable law;
- support reliable judicial statistics and evidence-based management;
- assess maturity and prioritize realistic improvements;
- establish trustworthy foundations for digital systems and responsible AI readiness.

## Scope

JDGF addresses governance arrangements, policies, roles, processes, controls, capabilities, evidence, and improvement mechanisms related to judicial data.

It is not a court-management system, database, analytics platform, predictive-justice solution, or artificial-intelligence product.

## Target users

- ministries of justice;
- courts, prosecution services, and registries;
- prison and correctional administrations;
- judicial statistical and planning services;
- information-system and data-protection functions;
- judicial training institutions;
- researchers and reform partners.

## Core principles

1. **Human and justice centered** — Data governance must serve people, rights, institutions, and the administration of justice.
2. **Governed before automated** — Digitalization and AI require trustworthy governance foundations.
3. **Institutional accountability** — Roles, decisions, controls, and exceptions must be explicit and traceable.
4. **Legality and proportionality** — Data practices must have legitimate purposes and proportionate safeguards.
5. **Quality and evidence** — Data must be fit for their judicial, administrative, and statistical uses.
6. **Security and confidentiality** — Sensitive judicial information requires risk-based protection.
7. **Interoperability with context** — Shared standards must respect legal mandates and institutional realities.
8. **Progressive maturity** — Improvement must be staged, realistic, measurable, and sustainable.
9. **Inclusion and capacity** — Governance arrangements must account for users, competencies, resources, and unequal institutional capabilities.
10. **Continuous improvement** — Framework application must generate learning, corrective action, and documented revision.

## Theory of change

If judicial institutions establish clear responsibilities, policies, controls, capabilities, and evidence for governing data, then data quality, protection, accessibility, and institutional use will improve. Better-governed data will support more reliable administration, statistics, accountability, reform decisions, digital transformation, and responsible AI readiness. This contributes to more effective, trustworthy, and human-centered justice institutions.

## Validation needs

Before v1.0, these foundations require:

- systematic literature and standards review;
- comparison with judicial and public-sector governance frameworks;
- expert and institutional review;
- testing through at least one documented case or pilot;
- documented limits and adaptation guidance.
