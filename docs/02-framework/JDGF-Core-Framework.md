# JDGF Core Framework

**Version:** 1.0 Working Draft  
**Status:** Pre-validation  
**Authoritative foundation:** [`../01-foundations/JDGF-Foundation.md`](../01-foundations/JDGF-Foundation.md)

## 1. Purpose

The Judicial Data Governance Framework (JDGF) provides a coherent institutional system for governing judicial data throughout its lifecycle. It translates the JDGF principles into governance domains, roles, decision rights, processes, controls, evidence, and measurable outcomes.

## 2. Framework architecture

JDGF consists of eight interdependent governance dimensions:

1. Leadership and institutional governance;
2. Legal, ethical, and rights-based governance;
3. Data architecture and interoperability;
4. Data quality and metadata;
5. Security, privacy, and continuity;
6. Data lifecycle and records governance;
7. Use, statistics, transparency, and responsible innovation;
8. Artificial intelligence readiness.

Artificial intelligence readiness is a distinct dimension because the JDGF theory of change requires institutions to establish human, institutional, governance, data, and digital preconditions before deploying AI. The dimension does not promote automation as an end in itself and does not imply that a more automated institution is necessarily more mature.

Each dimension is described through objectives, capabilities, responsibilities, required evidence, controls, indicators, and maturity expectations.

## 3. Operating model

JDGF applies the following governance cycle:

**Direct → Organize → Control → Measure → Improve**

- **Direct:** establish mandate, principles, policies, decision rights, and priorities.
- **Organize:** assign owners, stewards, custodians, committees, and operational responsibilities.
- **Control:** implement procedures, standards, safeguards, approvals, and traceability mechanisms.
- **Measure:** collect evidence, assess performance, evaluate risks, and calculate maturity.
- **Improve:** prioritize corrective actions, allocate resources, monitor delivery, and revise governance arrangements.

## 4. Governance layers

JDGF distinguishes four layers:

- **Strategic governance:** institutional mandate, accountability, policy, resources, risk appetite, and oversight.
- **Tactical governance:** standards, coordination, portfolio management, data domains, and improvement plans.
- **Operational governance:** daily data creation, validation, access, exchange, retention, protection, and use.
- **Assurance:** audit, assessment, evidence review, performance monitoring, and independent validation.

## 5. Core roles

| Role | Principal responsibility |
|---|---|
| Judicial governing authority | Approves direction, mandate, and accountability arrangements |
| Executive sponsor | Secures resources and institutional support |
| Data governance council | Coordinates decisions across judicial bodies and data domains |
| Chief data or designated governance lead | Maintains the governance system and improvement roadmap |
| Data owner | Accountable for a defined judicial data domain |
| Data steward | Maintains definitions, quality rules, metadata, and issue resolution |
| Data custodian | Implements technical controls, storage, protection, and availability |
| Records and archives authority | Governs retention, preservation, disposition, and evidentiary integrity |
| Security and privacy functions | Define and oversee safeguards, lawful access, incident response, and rights protection |
| AI governance or designated multidisciplinary body | Reviews AI readiness, use cases, impacts, controls, monitoring, and exit conditions |
| Internal audit or assurance function | Provides independent evaluation of design and effectiveness |
| Data users | Use data lawfully, responsibly, and according to approved purposes |

One person may perform several roles in a small institution, but accountability and conflicts of interest must remain explicit.

## 6. Decision rights

Institutions implementing JDGF must formally define who may:

- approve data policies and standards;
- designate authoritative data sources;
- approve data collection and exchange;
- define classifications and access conditions;
- accept data-related risks;
- authorize retention and disposition;
- approve publication and statistical release;
- authorize, suspend, or retire high-risk analytics or AI use;
- validate assessment results and improvement plans.

## 7. Required governance instruments

A conforming implementation should progressively establish:

- a data governance charter;
- a governance council mandate;
- an inventory of data domains and authoritative sources;
- a role and responsibility matrix;
- a controlled glossary and metadata catalogue;
- data quality standards and issue-management procedures;
- classification, access, sharing, retention, and publication policies;
- risk, incident, continuity, and assurance mechanisms;
- an inventory and governance process for AI use cases and systems;
- a maturity assessment and improvement roadmap.

## 8. Control model

Every JDGF control should specify:

1. objective;
2. accountable owner;
3. implementation procedure;
4. expected evidence;
5. review frequency;
6. performance indicator;
7. escalation route;
8. related legal or policy requirement.

Controls are evaluated for both **design** and **operating effectiveness**.

## 9. Performance model

JDGF distinguishes:

- **Capability indicators:** whether required governance mechanisms exist;
- **Performance indicators:** whether they operate consistently and produce results;
- **Risk indicators:** whether exposure is increasing or exceeding tolerance;
- **Outcome indicators:** whether governance improves reliability, protection, transparency, institutional performance, and human-centred justice outcomes.

## 10. Integration with other JDGF components

- The **Maturity Model** describes progressive capability levels.
- The **Assessment Methodology** defines evidence collection and scoring.
- The documentary **Assessment Instrument** operationalizes the maturity model and is part of the JDGF v1.0 scope.
- Software, automated platforms, and digital products implementing the instrument remain outside the JDGF v1.0 scope.
- The **Implementation Guide** defines adoption and improvement stages.
- The **Glossary** provides controlled definitions.
- The **References and Standards Mapping** documents scientific and normative alignment.
- The **Validation Plan** defines the process for empirical and expert validation.

## 11. Conformance

An institution may claim alignment with JDGF only when it:

- identifies the scope of assessment;
- documents evidence for each applicable capability;
- reports exclusions and limitations;
- applies the approved scoring method;
- validates results through accountable governance bodies;
- maintains a time-bound improvement plan.

JDGF alignment is not equivalent to legal compliance or certification unless an authorized certification scheme is subsequently established.

## 12. Status and evolution

This document is a working draft for JDGF v1.0. Dimensions, controls, indicators, and scoring rules must be refined through literature review, expert review, institutional pilots, and documented change control.
