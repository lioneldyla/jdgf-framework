# JDGF Framework Architecture

## Status

**Working Draft — architecture to validate before JDGF v1.0**

## Purpose

This document defines the organizing architecture of the JDGF. It is the authoritative map for future framework content and prevents independent or duplicative structures from emerging across the repository.

## Proposed dimensions

1. **Strategy and Institutional Alignment** — mandate, objectives, value, stakeholder alignment, and governance sponsorship.
2. **Leadership, Roles, and Accountability** — decision rights, ownership, stewardship, committees, escalation, and segregation of duties.
3. **Legal, Ethical, and Rights Safeguards** — legality, purpose limitation, privacy, confidentiality, fairness, transparency, and remedies.
4. **Data Architecture, Metadata, and Interoperability** — authoritative sources, models, vocabularies, lineage, exchange, and standards.
5. **Data Quality and Records Lifecycle** — quality requirements, validation, correction, retention, preservation, disposal, and archival continuity.
6. **Access, Security, and Resilience** — classification, authorization, monitoring, incident response, continuity, and recovery.
7. **Judicial Statistics, Use, and Decision Support** — statistical production, analytical use, publication, interpretation, and evidence-based management.
8. **Capabilities, Culture, and Change** — competencies, training, operating capacity, incentives, communication, and adoption.
9. **Risk, Assurance, and Performance** — risk management, controls, audit, indicators, compliance evidence, and improvement monitoring.
10. **Digital Transformation and Responsible AI Readiness** — prerequisites, data fitness, human oversight, impact assessment, accountability, and lifecycle governance.

## Common structure for every dimension

Each dimension must eventually specify:

- purpose and expected institutional outcome;
- scope and boundaries;
- sub-dimensions;
- accountable actors and supporting roles;
- required policies and processes;
- minimum controls;
- evidence and indicators;
- maturity criteria;
- risks and dependencies;
- implementation guidance;
- references.

## Governance actors

The role model should accommodate, according to institutional context:

- judicial and ministerial leadership;
- data-governance body or steering committee;
- data owners;
- data stewards;
- records and archives officers;
- statistical services;
- information-system and cybersecurity teams;
- legal, privacy, ethics, and compliance functions;
- operational judicial actors;
- internal audit and assurance;
- external oversight and authorized partners.

## Control model

Controls should be defined as verifiable institutional practices rather than abstract recommendations. Every control should identify:

- objective;
- accountable role;
- implementation evidence;
- frequency or trigger;
- risk addressed;
- maturity level at which it is expected.

## Architecture decisions still required

- validate or revise the ten dimensions through literature and stakeholder review;
- define authoritative terminology for ownership and stewardship in judicial settings;
- decide whether records management is a standalone dimension or remains integrated with lifecycle management;
- align assessment criteria with the maturity model;
- document contextual adaptation without weakening minimum safeguards.
