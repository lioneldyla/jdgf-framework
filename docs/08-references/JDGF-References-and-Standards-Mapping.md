# JDGF References and Standards Mapping

**Version:** 1.0 Candidate  
**Status:** Scientifically revised; clause-level verification and full systematic review pending

## 1. Purpose

This document records the principal bodies of knowledge, standards, scholarly sources, and institutional instruments relevant to the Judicial Data Governance Framework (JDGF). It supports traceability and comparison. Inclusion does not imply endorsement, certification, equivalence, or permission to reproduce protected standards.

## 2. Referencing rules

JDGF references must:

- identify the exact title, issuing body, edition or publication year, and persistent identifier where available;
- distinguish normative standards from guidance, professional bodies of knowledge, legislation, and peer-reviewed research;
- cite only source content that has been verified;
- avoid reproducing copyrighted clauses or proprietary framework content;
- document whether a JDGF requirement is aligned, adapted, extended, or distinct;
- record jurisdictional and institutional limitations.

## 5. Scientific basis for the scoring model

The JDGF scoring system is informed by capability-maturity and evidence-assessment traditions, but it is a distinct judicial data-governance model. Its scientific validation must address:

- content validity of dimensions, attributes, items, and critical controls;
- response-process validity and interpretability;
- inter-rater reliability and moderation;
- stability and sensitivity to change;
- relationships with external institutional indicators;
- feasibility and fairness across diverse judicial contexts;
- non-compensatory treatment of critical rights, integrity, security, and continuity failures.

The model is formative; therefore high internal consistency is neither required nor sufficient. Validation evidence must be accumulated through expert review and real institutional pilots.

## 6. Literature-review protocol

A full review before stable release should search multidisciplinary databases and institutional repositories using combinations of terms relating to judicial administration, courts, prosecution, prisons, data governance, data quality, records, statistics, privacy, security, interoperability, AI governance, capability maturity, and public-sector transformation.

The review must document:

1. databases and repositories searched;
2. exact search strings and dates;
3. inclusion and exclusion criteria;
4. language and jurisdiction coverage;
5. screening and extraction procedures;
6. quality appraisal;
7. synthesis method;
8. African and Global South evidence gaps;
9. conflicts, limitations, and update procedures.

## 7. Clause-level mapping backlog

Before stable release, each normative JDGF requirement should receive an identifier and a mapping record containing:

- exact source and edition;
- verified clause, principle, or section reference;
- relationship type: aligned, adapted, extended, or distinct;
- judicial-sector rationale;
- legal and jurisdictional constraints;
- reviewer and verification date.

Where a protected standard is not fully accessible, the mapping must remain at a verified public-summary level or be completed by an authorized reviewer with lawful access.

## 8. Limitations and currency

This revision verifies the principal editions and public descriptions available as of **28 July 2026**. Standards and institutional instruments change. Notably, ISO/IEC 38505-1 Edition 2 was in the final publication stage in July 2026; the repository must verify its final publication status and update the crosswalk before JDGF v1.0 is tagged.

This document is not yet a completed systematic review, and it does not claim clause-level equivalence with any external framework.
