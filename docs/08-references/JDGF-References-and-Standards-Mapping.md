# JDGF References and Standards Mapping

**Version:** 1.0 Candidate  
**Status:** Scientifically revised; clause-level verification and full systematic review pending  
**Canonical bibliography:** [`../../research/bibliography.md`](../../research/bibliography.md)

## 1. Purpose

This document maps the principal reference families to the eight JDGF dimensions. Full bibliographic records, annotations, evidence classes, and verification status are maintained in the canonical bibliography. This document should not duplicate that register.

Inclusion does not imply endorsement, certification, equivalence, or permission to reproduce protected standards.

## 2. Referencing rules

JDGF references must:

- identify the exact title, issuing body, edition or publication year, and persistent identifier where available;
- distinguish normative standards, legislation, institutional guidance, professional bodies of knowledge, scholarly research, and comparative cases;
- use identifiers from `research/bibliography.md`;
- cite only source content that has been verified;
- avoid reproducing copyrighted clauses or proprietary framework content;
- record whether a JDGF requirement is aligned, adapted, extended, or distinct;
- record jurisdictional and institutional limitations;
- flag incomplete or provisional bibliographic metadata before release.

## 4. Cross-dimensional contextual evidence

The following references support interpretation across several dimensions:

- **BIB-SCH-001** supports comparative judicial-data governance, transparency, use controls, privacy, fairness, and judicial independence.
- **BIB-SCH-002** supports African interoperability and institutional-integration analysis.
- **BIB-SCH-003** supports infrastructure and capacity constraints in African courts, subject to completion of bibliographic metadata.
- **BIB-INST-007** supports the African judicial-AI context, subject to completion of publication metadata.
- **BIB-CASE-001** supports staged digital transformation and interoperability but must not be transferred uncritically to lower-resource contexts.

## 5. Scientific basis for the scoring model

The JDGF scoring system is informed by capability-maturity and evidence-assessment traditions, but it is a distinct formative judicial data-governance model. Its validation must address:

- content validity of dimensions, attributes, items, and critical controls;
- response-process validity and interpretability;
- inter-rater reliability and moderation;
- stability and sensitivity to change;
- relationships with external institutional indicators;
- feasibility and fairness across diverse judicial contexts;
- non-compensatory treatment of critical rights, integrity, security, continuity, and unauthorized-AI failures.

High internal consistency is neither required nor sufficient because the model is formative. Validation evidence must be accumulated through structured expert review and authorized institutional pilots.

## 6. Requirement-level mapping record

Before stable release, every normative JDGF requirement should receive a stable identifier and a mapping record containing:

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| JDGF requirement ID | Stable identifier and document location |
| Bibliography ID | One or more identifiers from the canonical register |
| Exact source | Title, edition, date, and persistent identifier where available |
| Source location | Verified clause, principle, section, or public-summary location |
| Relationship | Aligned / Adapted / Extended / Distinct |
| Judicial rationale | Why the source is applicable or requires adaptation |
| Legal limitation | Jurisdictional or institutional constraints |
| Verification | Reviewer, verification date, and evidence-access level |

Where a protected standard is not fully accessible, mapping must remain at a verified public-summary level or be completed by an authorized reviewer with lawful access.

## 7. Literature-review protocol

The full review should search multidisciplinary databases and institutional repositories using combinations of terms relating to:

- judicial administration, courts, prosecution, corrections, and legal aid;
- data governance, data quality, records, metadata, statistics, privacy, security, and interoperability;
- artificial intelligence governance and judicial AI;
- capability maturity, organizational capacity, and public-sector transformation;
- African, Global South, civil-law, common-law, and hybrid legal contexts.

The review must document:

1. databases and repositories searched;
2. exact search strings and dates;
3. inclusion and exclusion criteria;
4. language and jurisdiction coverage;
5. screening and extraction procedures;
6. quality appraisal;
7. synthesis method;
8. African and Global South evidence gaps;
9. conflicts, limitations, and update procedures.

## 9. Limitations and currency

This mapping is a structured scientific foundation, not a completed systematic review or a claim of clause-level equivalence. Standards and institutional instruments change. Bibliographic currency must be checked at each release candidate and recorded in the canonical bibliography.
