# JDGF Maturity Model

**Version:** 1.0 Working Draft  
**Status:** Pre-validation

## 1. Purpose

The JDGF Maturity Model measures the institutional capability to govern judicial data consistently, lawfully, securely, and effectively. It supports diagnosis, prioritization, monitoring, and comparison over time. It is not a certification scheme.

## 2. Maturity levels

| Level | Name | Description |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Initial | Practices are informal, reactive, fragmented, and highly dependent on individuals. |
| 2 | Emerging | Responsibilities and recurring practices are beginning to be defined, but coverage and consistency remain limited. |
| 3 | Defined | Policies, roles, processes, standards, and evidence requirements are formally established and broadly implemented. |
| 4 | Managed | Controls are measured, risks are monitored, performance is reviewed, and corrective action is systematically governed. |
| 5 | Adaptive | Governance is continuously improved using evidence, institutional learning, foresight, and controlled innovation. |

## 3. Assessment criteria

Each JDGF dimension is assessed against six capability attributes:

1. **Mandate and policy** — formal authority, approved rules, and scope;
2. **Roles and accountability** — ownership, stewardship, decision rights, and escalation;
3. **Processes and controls** — documented and repeatable operating mechanisms;
4. **Information and evidence** — records demonstrating implementation and traceability;
5. **Measurement and assurance** — indicators, review, audit, and risk monitoring;
6. **Improvement and adaptation** — remediation, learning, capability development, and controlled change.

## 4. Scoring scale

Each criterion is scored from 0 to 5:

- **0 — Not present:** no credible evidence;
- **1 — Initial:** isolated or ad hoc evidence;
- **2 — Emerging:** partial implementation with material gaps;
- **3 — Defined:** formally established and substantially implemented;
- **4 — Managed:** measured, reviewed, and consistently effective;
- **5 — Adaptive:** continuously improved and demonstrably responsive to change.

Half scores may be used only when the assessment protocol explicitly permits them and the evidence justifies the distinction.

## 5. Evidence rule

A score must not be awarded solely on the basis of declarations or unpublished intentions. Evidence should demonstrate both:

- **design:** the capability is formally established and suitable for its objective;
- **operation:** the capability is implemented and functioning within the assessment period.

Examples include approved policies, role appointments, registers, system records, meeting decisions, monitoring reports, issue logs, audit results, training records, and tested procedures.

## 6. Dimension score

The initial scoring method uses the arithmetic mean of applicable capability attributes:

`Dimension score = Sum of attribute scores / Number of applicable attributes`

A dimension maturity level is determined by the rounded-down score, subject to the following safeguards:

- no dimension may reach Level 3 without approved policy, assigned accountability, and repeatable processes;
- no dimension may reach Level 4 without measured performance and evidence of corrective action;
- no dimension may reach Level 5 without sustained improvement evidence and controlled adaptation;
- critical legal, rights, security, or integrity failures may cap the dimension score.

## 7. Overall maturity

The overall JDGF score is the mean of applicable dimension scores. The overall level is the rounded-down result, but it cannot exceed the lowest score among any dimensions designated as critical for the assessment scope.

Institutions must report both the overall result and the complete dimension profile. A single aggregate score must not conceal material weaknesses.

## 8. Confidence rating

Each dimension receives an evidence-confidence rating:

- **High:** evidence is current, complete, independently verifiable, and representative;
- **Moderate:** evidence is credible but incomplete or based on limited sampling;
- **Low:** evidence is weak, outdated, inconsistent, or primarily declarative.

Low-confidence results must be clearly flagged and followed by additional evidence collection.

## 9. Assessment outputs

A maturity assessment should produce:

- scope and institutional context;
- scores by dimension and attribute;
- evidence register;
- confidence ratings;
- critical risks and gaps;
- strengths and transferable practices;
- prioritized improvement roadmap;
- target maturity and review date.

## 10. Progression principles

Progression should be risk-based rather than score-driven. Institutions should prioritize capabilities that protect rights, judicial integrity, continuity, data reliability, and lawful use before pursuing advanced analytics or automation.

## 11. Validation requirements

Before JDGF v1.0 is declared stable, this model must be tested for:

- clarity of criteria;
- inter-rater reliability;
- internal consistency;
- feasibility in institutions of different sizes and resource levels;
- sensitivity to meaningful improvement;
- resistance to unsupported score inflation.
