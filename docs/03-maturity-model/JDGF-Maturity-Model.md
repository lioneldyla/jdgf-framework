# JDGF Maturity Model

**Version:** 1.0 Candidate  
**Status:** Scientifically revised; empirical validation pending

## 1. Purpose

The JDGF Maturity Model measures an institution's capability to govern judicial data consistently, lawfully, securely, transparently, and effectively. It supports diagnosis, prioritization, monitoring, and comparison over time. It is not a certification scheme and must not be presented as one without an authorized conformity-assessment mechanism.

## 2. Measurement model

JDGF uses a **formative capability model**: the assessed attributes jointly constitute institutional maturity. They are not assumed to be interchangeable manifestations of a single latent trait. Consequently, internal-consistency statistics may be informative for item review but cannot, by themselves, establish validity.

The model combines:

- seven governance dimensions;
- six recurring capability attributes;
- evidence-based item scores from 0 to 5;
- critical-control gates;
- evidence-confidence ratings;
- explicit uncertainty and limitation reporting.

## 3. Maturity levels

| Level | Name | Description |
|---:|---|---|
| 0 | Absent | The capability is not established, cannot be evidenced, or is materially inapplicable only where a documented exclusion has been approved. |
| 1 | Initial | Practices are informal, reactive, fragmented, and highly dependent on individuals. |
| 2 | Emerging | Responsibilities and recurring practices are beginning to be defined, but coverage and consistency remain limited. |
| 3 | Defined | Policies, roles, processes, standards, and evidence requirements are formally established and substantially implemented. |
| 4 | Managed | Controls are measured, risks are monitored, performance is reviewed, and corrective action is systematically governed. |
| 5 | Adaptive | Governance is continuously improved using evidence, institutional learning, foresight, and controlled innovation. |

Level 0 is an item score and reporting category. Institutional maturity claims normally use Levels 1–5 after applicability has been resolved.

## 4. Capability attributes

Each JDGF dimension is assessed against six attributes:

1. **Mandate and policy** — formal authority, approved rules, scope, and accountability;
2. **Roles and accountability** — ownership, stewardship, decision rights, segregation of duties, and escalation;
3. **Processes and controls** — documented, repeatable, proportionate, and implemented operating mechanisms;
4. **Information and evidence** — current records demonstrating implementation, traceability, and coverage;
5. **Measurement and assurance** — indicators, review, testing, audit, risk monitoring, and independent challenge;
6. **Improvement and adaptation** — remediation, learning, capability development, controlled change, and sustained improvement.

## 5. Item scoring anchors

Each applicable item is scored from 0 to 5:

- **0 — Absent:** no credible evidence, or the expected capability is not established;
- **1 — Initial:** isolated, informal, person-dependent, or reactive practice;
- **2 — Emerging:** partially defined or implemented, with material gaps in scope, consistency, or evidence;
- **3 — Defined:** formally approved, assigned, repeatable, and substantially implemented across the assessed scope;
- **4 — Managed:** implementation is measured, tested, reviewed, and demonstrably effective, with corrective action;
- **5 — Adaptive:** effectiveness is sustained over time and the capability is systematically improved in response to evidence, risk, and change.

Scores must reflect the **lowest fully evidenced anchor**. Aspirations, draft documents, isolated examples, and uncorroborated statements do not justify higher scores. Half scores are prohibited in the v1.0 candidate model to improve inter-rater consistency.

## 6. Evidence sufficiency

Every score requires traceable evidence of both:

- **design adequacy:** the capability is formally established and suitable for its objective;
- **operating effectiveness:** the capability functions in practice during the assessment period.

Evidence should be evaluated for authenticity, recency, relevance, completeness, coverage, and independence. Typical sources include approved policies, appointments, registers, system records, meeting decisions, monitoring reports, issue logs, audit results, training records, tested procedures, and direct observations.

A score above 2 normally requires approved and current design evidence. A score above 3 requires operating evidence covering a representative period. A score of 5 requires evidence of at least one completed improvement cycle and sustained effectiveness.

## 7. Applicability

An item may be marked **Not Applicable (N/A)** only when:

1. the exclusion is justified by the assessment scope, legal framework, institutional mandate, or absence of the relevant activity;
2. the rationale is documented;
3. the assessment lead approves the exclusion; and
4. the exclusion does not remove an inherent judicial obligation or critical safeguard.

N/A items are removed from the denominator and reported separately. Missing evidence is never N/A.

## 8. Item and attribute aggregation

For each capability attribute:

`Attribute score = Sum of applicable item scores / Number of applicable items`

For each dimension:

`Dimension score = Sum of applicable attribute scores / Number of applicable attributes`

Scores are reported to two decimal places. The descriptive maturity level is the integer part of the score, subject to the gates and caps below. Aggregation is used for prioritization and monitoring; it does not override a material adverse finding.

## 9. Critical-control gates and caps

The following rules prevent compensatory averaging:

- no dimension may reach Level 3 without approved policy, assigned accountability, and repeatable processes;
- no dimension may reach Level 4 without performance measures, periodic review, tested operating effectiveness, and documented corrective action;
- no dimension may reach Level 5 without sustained improvement evidence, controlled adaptation, and independent challenge;
- a critical legal, fundamental-rights, judicial-integrity, security, continuity, evidentiary-integrity, or unauthorized-use failure caps the affected dimension at Level 2 until remediated and verified;
- an unresolved critical finding caps the overall institutional maturity at Level 2;
- a low-confidence dimension cannot support a maturity claim above Level 2 unless additional evidence is obtained.

Critical items must be identified in the assessment instrument before fieldwork and justified for the institutional scope.

## 10. Overall maturity profile

The overall numerical score is the mean of applicable dimension scores:

`Overall score = Sum of applicable dimension scores / Number of applicable dimensions`

The overall descriptive level is the integer part of the overall score, but cannot exceed:

- the lowest level among dimensions designated as critical for the assessment scope;
- any cap produced by an unresolved critical finding;
- the level permitted by evidence confidence.

Institutions must report the complete seven-dimension profile, score distribution, critical findings, exclusions, and confidence ratings. A single aggregate score must never be reported alone.

## 11. Evidence-confidence rating

Each dimension receives a confidence rating:

- **High:** evidence is current, complete, independently verifiable, representative, and triangulated;
- **Moderate:** evidence is credible but incomplete, unevenly sampled, or only partly independently verified;
- **Low:** evidence is weak, outdated, inconsistent, narrowly sampled, or primarily declarative.

The report must explain confidence judgments and their effect on interpretation.

## 12. Change measurement

Progress assessments should compare like-for-like scope and retain the prior evidence register. A change is considered meaningful only when:

- the underlying evidence has changed;
- the improvement is operational rather than merely documentary;
- the change survives moderation and applicable gates;
- the assessment explains any change in scope or scoring interpretation.

## 13. Validation strategy

Before JDGF v1.0 is declared empirically validated, the scoring model must be tested for:

- content validity through structured expert review;
- response-process validity through cognitive interviews with assessors and institutional respondents;
- inter-rater reliability using independently scored cases and an appropriate agreement statistic;
- test-retest stability where institutional conditions have not materially changed;
- construct and criterion-related evidence using expected relationships with external indicators where suitable;
- feasibility, burden, missingness, and evidence availability;
- sensitivity to meaningful institutional improvement;
- resistance to unsupported score inflation and gaming;
- fairness across institution types, sizes, resource levels, and legal contexts.

Because JDGF is formative, factor analysis and coefficient alpha must not be treated as sole or decisive validation tests.

## 14. Minimum validation thresholds for release decisions

The following are provisional release criteria and must themselves be reviewed after pilot data are available:

- expert content-validity review with documented item-level decisions;
- at least two independent raters for a representative subset of cases;
- weighted agreement or intraclass correlation of at least 0.70 for dimension scores as an initial acceptability threshold;
- no systematic scoring bias greater than 0.50 points between assessors without documented explanation;
- at least 80% of applicable items assessable with credible evidence in the pilot context;
- median completion burden judged feasible by participating institutions;
- all critical items demonstrating clear interpretation and acceptable agreement.

Failure to meet a threshold requires item revision, scoring clarification, additional assessor training, or further piloting; it does not justify suppressing results.

## 15. Status statement

The revised model has stronger theoretical and procedural safeguards, but no real institutional dataset has yet been supplied to this repository. Therefore its scoring system is **methodologically specified, not empirically validated**. Any pilot illustration must be labelled simulated until collected from an authorized institution under the approved protocol.
