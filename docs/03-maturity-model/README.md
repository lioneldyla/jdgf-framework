# JDGF Maturity Model

## Status

**Working Draft — requires empirical validation**

## Purpose

The maturity model helps judicial institutions understand their current governance capability, define a realistic target state, prioritize improvements, and track progress over time.

## Levels

| Level | Name | Institutional characteristics |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ad hoc | Responsibilities and practices are informal, fragmented, reactive, and weakly evidenced. |
| 2 | Defined | Initial policies, roles, inventories, and repeatable procedures exist but are unevenly applied. |
| 3 | Managed | Responsibilities are operational, controls are monitored, quality and risk indicators are used, and evidence is retained. |
| 4 | Integrated | Governance is coordinated across functions and systems, supported by interoperability, assurance, and continuous improvement. |
| 5 | Adaptive | Governance is institutionally embedded, outcome-oriented, resilient, regularly evaluated, and capable of supporting responsible innovation. |

## Assessment principles

- Maturity is assessed per dimension, not only through one global score.
- Documentary claims require verifiable evidence.
- Higher levels require sustained practice, not merely policy publication.
- Institutions may have different maturity levels across dimensions.
- The target level must reflect mandate, risk, resources, and institutional context.
- Level 5 is not synonymous with maximum automation or AI use.

## Minimum evidence categories

- approved governance instruments;
- assigned and accepted responsibilities;
- operational records and registers;
- monitoring data and indicators;
- audit or review evidence;
- corrective-action records;
- training and capacity evidence;
- stakeholder or user feedback.

## Scoring approach to develop

The v1.0 assessment method must define:

- criteria for each dimension and level;
- evidence thresholds;
- treatment of partially met criteria;
- weighting rules, if any;
- confidence and evidence-quality ratings;
- validation and moderation procedures;
- reporting rules that avoid false precision.

## Safeguard

The maturity model is an improvement instrument, not a ranking mechanism for publicly comparing institutions without agreed methodology, context, and safeguards.
