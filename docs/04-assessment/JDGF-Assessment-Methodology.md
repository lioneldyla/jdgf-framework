# JDGF Assessment Methodology

**Version:** 1.0 Working Draft  
**Status:** Pre-validation

## 1. Purpose

This methodology defines how to plan, conduct, document, validate, and report a JDGF maturity assessment.

## 2. Assessment principles

Assessments must be:

- evidence-based;
- proportionate to institutional context and risk;
- transparent and reproducible;
- respectful of confidentiality and judicial independence;
- inclusive of relevant institutional actors;
- explicit about limitations and uncertainty;
- oriented toward improvement rather than blame.

## 3. Assessment types

- **Baseline assessment:** establishes the initial maturity profile.
- **Targeted assessment:** examines selected dimensions, domains, or institutions.
- **Progress assessment:** measures implementation since a prior assessment.
- **Independent assessment:** performed by assessors with organizational independence.
- **Self-assessment:** performed internally and subject to governance validation.

## 4. Assessment process

### Step 1 — Mandate and scope

Define the sponsor, objectives, institutions, data domains, period, exclusions, legal constraints, deliverables, and intended users.

### Step 2 — Assessment planning

Prepare the assessment plan, stakeholder map, interview schedule, document request, sampling strategy, security arrangements, and conflict-of-interest declarations.

### Step 3 — Evidence collection

Use multiple methods where appropriate:

- document and policy review;
- structured interviews;
- workshops;
- observation;
- data and system sampling;
- control walkthroughs;
- register, log, and record inspection;
- survey instruments.

### Step 4 — Evidence evaluation

For each criterion, record:

- evidence identifier and source;
- relevance;
- date and coverage period;
- authenticity and reliability;
- design evidence;
- operating evidence;
- identified gaps;
- provisional score;
- confidence rating.

### Step 5 — Scoring and moderation

Assessors apply the JDGF Maturity Model. Scores should be moderated by at least two reviewers for high-impact assessments. Disagreements must be documented and resolved through evidence, not hierarchy.

### Step 6 — Validation

Present preliminary findings to accountable stakeholders for factual verification. Stakeholders may correct facts or provide additional evidence, but they may not suppress supported findings.

### Step 7 — Reporting

Issue a report containing scope, method, maturity profile, evidence confidence, strengths, gaps, risks, recommendations, and a prioritized roadmap.

### Step 8 — Follow-up

Assign improvement actions, owners, deadlines, resources, indicators, and review dates. Progress must be monitored through the appropriate governance body.

## 5. Sampling

Sampling must reflect risk and materiality. It should cover critical judicial data, high-volume processes, sensitive records, major systems, inter-institutional exchanges, and known problem areas. The report must explain the sample and its limitations.

## 6. Evidence hierarchy

Stronger evidence generally includes:

1. verified operational records and system-generated evidence;
2. approved and current institutional documents;
3. consistent evidence from multiple independent sources;
4. direct observation or tested control execution;
5. interviews and declarations corroborated by evidence;
6. uncorroborated statements or future intentions.

The hierarchy is contextual and does not replace professional judgment.

## 7. Assessment safeguards

Assessors must protect sensitive judicial information, minimize unnecessary personal-data collection, follow access restrictions, maintain secure working papers, and apply retention and disposal rules to assessment evidence.

## 8. Rating findings

Findings should be classified by impact and urgency:

- **Critical:** immediate threat to rights, judicial integrity, legality, security, continuity, or evidentiary reliability;
- **High:** major weakness requiring senior action and time-bound remediation;
- **Moderate:** significant capability gap with manageable near-term risk;
- **Low:** limited weakness or improvement opportunity;
- **Good practice:** effective capability suitable for preservation or replication.

## 9. Report structure

1. Executive summary;
2. Mandate and scope;
3. Institutional context;
4. Method and limitations;
5. Overall maturity profile;
6. Findings by JDGF dimension;
7. Critical risks;
8. Recommendations;
9. Improvement roadmap;
10. Evidence-confidence statement;
11. Appendices and evidence register.

## 10. Quality assurance

The assessment team should complete an internal quality review covering traceability, scoring consistency, confidentiality, conflicts of interest, unsupported claims, and alignment between findings and recommendations.
