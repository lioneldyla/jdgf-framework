**Version:** 1.0 Working Draft  
**Status:** Pilot instrument

## Scoring instructions

Score each question from 0 to 5 according to the JDGF Maturity Model. Record the evidence reference, finding, confidence rating, and proposed action. A score without traceable evidence is invalid.

## Dimension 1 — Leadership and institutional governance

1. Is there a formally approved judicial data governance mandate?
2. Are executive sponsorship and institutional accountability explicit?
3. Is a data governance council or equivalent body operating regularly?
4. Are data owners, stewards, and custodians assigned for critical domains?
5. Are decision rights and escalation routes documented?
6. Are governance resources, competencies, and training planned?
7. Are risks, performance, and improvement actions reported to leadership?

## Dimension 2 — Legal, ethical, and rights-based governance

1. Are applicable legal and regulatory obligations identified and maintained?
2. Are collection, access, exchange, publication, and secondary use tied to lawful purposes?
3. Are privacy, rights, and ethical impacts assessed for high-risk activities?
4. Are confidentiality, due process, equality, and judicial independence protected?
5. Are complaints, redress, and accountability mechanisms available?
6. Are high-risk analytics and AI uses subject to authorization and oversight?

## Dimension 3 — Data architecture and interoperability

1. Is there an inventory of judicial data domains, systems, and exchanges?
2. Are authoritative data sources formally designated?
3. Are common definitions, identifiers, models, and exchange standards used?
4. Are data lineage and dependencies documented for critical information?
5. Are interfaces and exchange agreements governed and tested?
6. Are architecture changes and legacy transitions controlled?

## Dimension 4 — Data quality and metadata

1. Are critical data elements identified and defined?
2. Is metadata documented and maintained in a controlled catalogue?
3. Are quality dimensions, rules, thresholds, and owners established?
4. Is quality monitored and reported at an appropriate frequency?
5. Are quality issues logged, prioritized, remediated, and escalated?
6. Is root-cause analysis used to prevent recurring problems?

## Dimension 5 — Security, privacy, and continuity

1. Is judicial data classified according to sensitivity and criticality?
2. Are access rights approved, reviewed, and based on least privilege?
3. Are secure storage, transmission, logging, and monitoring controls operating?
4. Are security and privacy incidents detected, reported, investigated, and remediated?
5. Are backup, recovery, and continuity arrangements tested?
6. Are third-party and inter-institutional risks assessed and controlled?

## Dimension 6 — Data lifecycle and records governance

1. Are lifecycle responsibilities and rules defined from creation to disposition?
2. Are records classifications and retention schedules approved and applied?
3. Are evidentiary integrity, authenticity, and chain of custody protected?
4. Are legal holds and preservation requirements implemented?
5. Are archival transfer and long-term preservation controlled?
6. Is disposition authorized, documented, and verifiable?

## Dimension 7 — Use, statistics, transparency, and responsible innovation

1. Are operational and secondary uses authorized and purpose-limited?
2. Are judicial statistics produced under documented quality and release procedures?
3. Are disclosure, anonymization, and publication risks assessed?
4. Are research access and data-sharing requests governed?
5. Are analytics and AI systems inventoried, documented, and risk-assessed?
6. Are outcomes, errors, bias, and unintended effects monitored?
7. Are transparency obligations balanced with confidentiality and rights?
