# JDGF Implementation Guide

## Status

**Working Draft — implementation patterns require field testing**

## Purpose

This guide will help judicial institutions translate JDGF assessment findings into proportionate, sequenced, and sustainable governance improvements.

## Implementation phases

### 1. Mobilize

- establish institutional sponsorship and mandate;
- define scope, objectives, decision rights, and reporting lines;
- identify priority data domains and material risks;
- appoint accountable owners and implementation coordination.

### 2. Diagnose

- conduct the JDGF assessment;
- establish the maturity baseline;
- identify legal, institutional, operational, technical, and capacity constraints;
- validate findings with responsible actors.

### 3. Design

- define target maturity by dimension;
- select priority policies, roles, processes, controls, and indicators;
- assign resources, owners, dependencies, and timelines;
- integrate safeguards for rights, confidentiality, security, and judicial independence.

### 4. Implement

- approve governance instruments;
- operationalize roles and forums;
- deploy priority controls and registers;
- improve metadata, quality, access, lifecycle, and evidence practices;
- train actors and manage organizational change.

### 5. Monitor and improve

- track indicators and corrective actions;
- review incidents, exceptions, risks, and evidence;
- reassess maturity periodically;
- document lessons and revise governance arrangements.

## Prioritization criteria

Actions should be ranked using:

- risk to rights, justice operations, confidentiality, and public trust;
- legal or regulatory obligation;
- institutional value and urgency;
- dependency on foundational capabilities;
- feasibility, resources, and sustainability;
- evidence strength and expected measurable outcome.

## Minimum implementation products

- governance mandate and charter;
- stakeholder and responsibility map;
- priority data-domain inventory;
- policy and control roadmap;
- risk and issue register;
- capability-development plan;
- monitoring indicators;
- decision and change log;
- periodic maturity review.

## Adaptation principle

JDGF defines common governance outcomes and minimum safeguards. Institutions may adapt structures, terminology, and sequencing to their legal system, mandate, scale, resources, and maturity, provided that adaptations are documented and do not weaken essential protections.
