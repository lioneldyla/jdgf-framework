# JDGF Glossary

**Version:** 1.0 Working Draft  
**Status:** Controlled terminology under review

This glossary provides authoritative working definitions for JDGF. Definitions must be revised when scientific review, applicable law, or institutional validation requires greater precision.

## Terms

**Accountability** — The obligation of an identified actor to explain and justify decisions, demonstrate performance, and accept appropriate consequences.

**Assessment evidence** — Verifiable information used to determine whether a JDGF capability is designed and operating effectively.

**Authoritative data source** — The formally designated source considered the official reference for a defined data element or domain.

**Capability** — A combination of mandate, people, roles, processes, information, controls, technology, and resources that enables an institution to achieve a governance objective.

**Critical data element** — A data element whose failure could materially affect rights, judicial decisions, operations, statistics, security, compliance, or public trust.

**Data architecture** — The organized description of data domains, structures, models, flows, systems, standards, and relationships.

**Data classification** — The assignment of categories to data according to sensitivity, criticality, legal requirements, and protection needs.

**Data custodian** — The role responsible for implementing technical and operational controls for storage, availability, protection, transmission, and recovery.

**Data domain** — A coherent area of institutional data associated with a judicial function, subject, process, or accountable owner.

**Data governance** — The system of authority, decision rights, accountability, policies, processes, controls, and oversight through which data is directed and managed.

**Data governance council** — A formally mandated body that coordinates and oversees data-governance decisions across institutions or domains.

**Data lifecycle** — The stages through which data passes from creation or acquisition to use, exchange, retention, preservation, archival transfer, and disposition.

**Data lineage** — The documented origin, movement, transformation, and use of data across processes and systems.

**Data owner** — The role accountable for decisions concerning a defined data domain, including quality, access, use, risk, and lifecycle requirements.

**Data quality** — The degree to which data is fit for its intended purpose, commonly assessed through accuracy, completeness, consistency, timeliness, validity, uniqueness, and integrity.

**Data steward** — The role responsible for maintaining definitions, metadata, quality rules, standards, issue resolution, and day-to-day governance within a data domain.

**Decision rights** — The formally assigned authority to make, approve, challenge, or escalate defined governance decisions.

**Evidence confidence** — The assessed strength, completeness, currency, and verifiability of evidence supporting a maturity result.

**Governance control** — A measure designed to direct behavior, reduce risk, ensure compliance, or provide evidence that a governance objective is achieved.

**Interoperability** — The ability of institutions, processes, and systems to exchange and meaningfully use data according to agreed legal, semantic, organizational, and technical conditions.

**Judicial data** — Data created, received, maintained, exchanged, or used by judicial institutions or in support of justice administration, subject to applicable legal and institutional boundaries.

**Judicial institution** — A court, prosecution service, ministry, judicial council, correctional body, registry, or other public entity performing or supporting justice functions, as defined in the relevant jurisdiction.

**Judicial statistics** — Aggregated or analytical information produced from judicial data to describe workloads, processes, outputs, outcomes, resources, or system performance.

**Metadata** — Structured information that describes the meaning, origin, format, ownership, quality, lineage, classification, and use conditions of data.

**Maturity** — The demonstrated degree to which a governance capability is established, implemented, measured, assured, and improved.

**Operating effectiveness** — The extent to which a control or capability functions consistently in practice during the period assessed.

**Records governance** — The authority, policies, responsibilities, and controls applied to records throughout their lifecycle to preserve authenticity, reliability, integrity, usability, and lawful disposition.

**Responsible innovation** — The controlled introduction of new methods or technologies through lawful purpose, risk assessment, rights safeguards, transparency, accountability, monitoring, and the capacity to stop or correct harmful use.

**Risk appetite** — The type and amount of data-related risk an institution is prepared to accept in pursuit of its objectives.

**Traceability** — The ability to reconstruct the origin, authorization, transformation, access, use, decision, or disposition associated with data.

## Terminology governance

New terms must be proposed with a definition, rationale, source, owner, and impact analysis. Approved changes must be reflected consistently throughout JDGF documentation and recorded in the changelog.
