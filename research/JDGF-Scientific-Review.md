# JDGF Scientific Review

**Version:** 1.0 Candidate  
**Review date:** 28 July 2026  
**Status:** Internal methodological review; independent peer review pending

## 1. Review scope

This review examines the conceptual coherence, scientific defensibility, measurement design, evidence rules, standards alignment, and validation readiness of the JDGF documentation prepared on branch `agent/prepare-jdgf-v1`.

It is an internal review of framework design. It is not an independent academic peer review, legal opinion, certification audit, or empirical validation.

## 2. Principal strengths

The framework has a clear institutional rather than technology-first orientation. It distinguishes strategic governance, operational responsibility, control, measurement, and assurance. The seven dimensions cover the principal governance concerns relevant to judicial data while preserving explicit attention to rights, judicial integrity, records, statistics, transparency, and responsible innovation.

The framework also correctly treats maturity assessment as evidence-based and improvement-oriented rather than as certification. The separation between score and evidence confidence is scientifically useful, as is the requirement to report a full dimension profile rather than only an aggregate score.

## 3. Principal scientific risks identified

### 3.1 Construct-definition risk

The original documentation described dimensions and capability attributes but did not explicitly identify whether the model was reflective or formative. Treating JDGF as a formative capability model is more defensible because governance capabilities jointly constitute maturity and need not correlate strongly.

### 3.2 Compensatory-scoring risk

An arithmetic mean could allow high scores in less critical areas to conceal severe weaknesses in legality, rights, security, continuity, or evidentiary integrity. Critical-control gates and score caps are therefore required.

### 3.3 Evidence-inflation risk

Without explicit anchors, assessors could award advanced scores for policies that exist only on paper. The revised model requires design adequacy and operating effectiveness and links Levels 4 and 5 to measured effectiveness and sustained improvement.

### 3.4 Applicability and missing-data risk

The original method did not fully distinguish genuine non-applicability from missing evidence. The revised model requires documented approval for exclusions and prohibits using N/A to avoid an inherent obligation.

### 3.5 Reliability risk

The pilot instrument contains broad questions that may be interpreted differently by assessors and respondents. A real pilot must include cognitive testing, independent scoring, moderation, and agreement analysis.

### 3.6 External-validity risk

The framework is intended for diverse judicial institutions, but no institutional dataset has yet been supplied. A single simulated case cannot establish transferability across courts, prosecution services, prisons, ministries, legal traditions, or resource settings.

### 3.7 Reference-traceability risk

The initial reference map named relevant families but lacked exact editions and persistent identifiers. The revised map verifies core editions and explicitly marks clause-level mapping as pending.

## 4. Revisions completed

The following methodological revisions have been committed:

- explicit formative measurement model;
- clarified Level 0 and Levels 1–5;
- anchored item scoring with no half scores;
- design and operating-effectiveness evidence rules;
- N/A governance and missing-evidence distinction;
- attribute, dimension, and overall aggregation formulas;
- non-compensatory critical-control gates and caps;
- evidence-confidence limits on claims;
- validation domains and provisional thresholds;
- verified core reference list;
- institutional pilot protocol;
- transparent simulated pilot;
- explicit statement that empirical validation remains pending.

## 5. Validity argument required for JDGF v1.0

A stable release should be supported by a documented validity argument covering:

1. **Content:** experts judge the dimensions, items, and critical controls relevant and sufficiently comprehensive.
2. **Response processes:** respondents and assessors understand items and scoring anchors as intended.
3. **Internal structure:** agreement patterns and score distributions behave consistently with the formative design, without relying improperly on a single internal-consistency coefficient.
4. **Relations to other variables:** scores show plausible relationships with independent indicators such as audit findings, data-quality incidents, continuity tests, or governance implementation status, where ethically and methodologically appropriate.
5. **Consequences:** assessment use improves prioritization without creating harmful incentives, concealment, discrimination, or unsupported claims.
6. **Generalizability:** interpretation remains defensible across institution types and contexts, with documented adaptations.

## 6. Minimum empirical work still required

Before the repository can claim that the scoring system is validated:

- conduct structured review by judicial, legal, data-governance, records, statistics, security, privacy, and measurement experts;
- perform cognitive interviews;
- execute at least one authorized feasibility pilot and preferably multiple heterogeneous pilots;
- obtain independent ratings for representative cases;
- calculate and interpret agreement statistics;
- revise ambiguous or low-agreement items;
- document burden, missingness, fairness, and institutional usefulness;
- repeat testing after material changes;
- publish an anonymized validation report.

## 7. Publication decision

The documentation may be released as a transparent **candidate or pre-validation version**. It should not be labelled a scientifically validated JDGF v1.0 until the real-world validation requirements have been satisfied.

A responsible release statement would distinguish:

- conceptual framework completion;
- methodological specification completion;
- reference verification status;
- simulated testing status;
- real institutional pilot status;
- independent review status;
- empirical validation status.

## 8. Conclusion

JDGF now has a stronger and more defensible scientific architecture. The remaining limitation is empirical rather than merely editorial: real institutional authorization, evidence, participants, independent ratings, and validation analysis are still required. The repository should preserve this distinction throughout publication and communication.
