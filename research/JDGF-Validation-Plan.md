# JDGF Validation Plan

**Version:** 1.0 Working Draft  
**Status:** Research protocol under development

## 1. Purpose

This plan defines the scientific and institutional validation activities required before JDGF v1.0 can be described as stable. Validation must test conceptual relevance, content validity, usability, scoring reliability, institutional feasibility, and practical value.

## 2. Validation questions

1. Do the JDGF dimensions adequately represent the governance capabilities required for judicial data?
2. Are the definitions, criteria, controls, and maturity levels clear and internally coherent?
3. Can independent assessors apply the framework consistently?
4. Is the framework usable in judicial institutions with different mandates, sizes, legal systems, and resource levels?
5. Does JDGF identify material risks and support actionable improvement?
6. Are rights, judicial independence, evidentiary integrity, privacy, security, and public accountability appropriately protected?
7. Does implementation produce observable governance and data-quality improvement?

## 3. Validation workstreams

### Workstream A — Literature and standards review

Conduct a documented review of peer-reviewed literature, standards, legal instruments, and institutional guidance. Produce a source matrix and clause-level mapping for material JDGF requirements.

### Workstream B — Conceptual and content validation

Use a multidisciplinary expert panel representing judicial administration, law, data governance, records, statistics, security, privacy, ethics, digital transformation, and research methodology.

Experts should rate each major component for relevance, clarity, completeness, feasibility, and judicial specificity. Proposed changes and dissenting views must be recorded.

### Workstream C — Cognitive testing

Test whether intended users interpret definitions, assessment questions, scoring anchors, and evidence requirements consistently. Revise ambiguous or overly technical language.

### Workstream D — Pilot assessments

Conduct pilot assessments in at least one judicial institution, and preferably several institutions with contrasting contexts. Each pilot should document scope, evidence availability, assessor effort, disputed scores, institutional response, and improvement usefulness.

### Workstream E — Reliability testing

Where feasible, use multiple assessors to evaluate the same evidence and examine inter-rater agreement. Review criteria that produce repeated disagreement or unsupported score variance.

### Workstream F — Practical outcome validation

Track selected implementation actions and reassess maturity and operational indicators after an appropriate period. Examine whether improvements are attributable, sustainable, and institutionally meaningful.

### Workstream G — Independent review

Submit the consolidated framework, evidence, limitations, and revision log to reviewers who were not responsible for the original design.

## 4. Participant selection

Validation participants should be selected transparently and should include diverse institutional roles, legal traditions, geographic contexts, levels of digital maturity, and resource environments. Conflicts of interest must be declared.

## 5. Data collection

Potential methods include:

- structured expert questionnaires;
- Delphi rounds or facilitated consensus workshops;
- interviews and focus groups;
- document and evidence review;
- observed assessment exercises;
- inter-rater scoring studies;
- implementation monitoring;
- post-pilot surveys and lessons-learned sessions.

## 6. Evaluation criteria

JDGF should be evaluated for:

- relevance;
- completeness;
- clarity;
- coherence;
- legal and ethical suitability;
- feasibility;
- scalability;
- cultural and institutional adaptability;
- assessment reliability;
- actionability;
- transparency;
- resistance to unsupported maturity claims.

## 7. Ethical and institutional safeguards

Validation must protect confidential judicial information, personal data, institutional security, participants, and judicial independence. Necessary approvals, consent arrangements, access controls, anonymization, retention, and publication restrictions must be documented.

## 8. Change-control process

Every material validation finding should be entered in a decision log containing:

- issue identifier;
- source and evidence;
- affected JDGF component;
- proposed revision;
- rationale;
- decision authority;
- accepted, rejected, or deferred status;
- version implemented.

## 9. Minimum release criteria for JDGF v1.0

JDGF v1.0 should not be declared stable until:

- the foundation, dimensions, maturity model, assessment method, and implementation guide are internally consistent;
- the literature and standards mapping is verified;
- expert content review is completed and documented;
- at least one institutional pilot is completed;
- scoring and evidence rules are revised based on pilot findings;
- known limitations are published;
- independent review is completed;
- versioning, licensing, contribution, and change-control arrangements are operational.

## 10. Planned validation outputs

- literature review and standards crosswalk;
- expert-panel report;
- pilot protocols and case studies;
- reliability analysis;
- revision and decision log;
- validation report;
- final JDGF v1.0 release recommendation.
