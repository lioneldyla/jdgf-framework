# JDGF Expert Review Packet

**Version:** 1.0 Candidate  
**Status:** Ready for structured expert review  
**Scope:** Judicial Data Governance Framework v1.0 candidate

## 1. Purpose

This packet supports structured multidisciplinary review of the JDGF conceptual architecture, eight governance dimensions, formative maturity model, documentary assessment instrument, critical-control gates, and implementation assumptions.

It is designed to collect evidence for content validity and practical relevance. It does not constitute empirical validation, certification, or institutional approval.

## 2. Recommended expert panel

The panel should collectively include experience in:

- judicial administration or court leadership;
- registry, records, archives, or judicial statistics;
- data governance and data quality;
- information security, privacy, and continuity;
- law, ethics, human rights, and judicial independence;
- artificial intelligence governance;
- research methods, measurement, or programme evaluation;
- African or other resource-constrained justice-system contexts.

A single expert is not expected to cover all areas. Reviewers must declare their areas of competence and any conflicts of interest.

## 4. Rating scale

Unless otherwise stated, use:

- **1 — Not acceptable:** fundamentally unclear, irrelevant, unsafe, or unsuitable;
- **2 — Major revision required:** important defects or omissions;
- **3 — Acceptable with revision:** broadly sound but needs clarification or strengthening;
- **4 — Strong:** relevant, clear, and practically suitable;
- **5 — Essential / exemplary:** indispensable and exceptionally well specified;
- **N/R — Not rated:** outside the reviewer's competence.

Every rating of 1 or 2 must include a reason and proposed correction. Ratings of 4 or 5 should identify the feature that should be preserved.

## 10. Context and fairness review

Reviewers should consider whether JDGF creates unfair disadvantages for:

- small rural courts;
- jurisdictions operating mainly on paper;
- institutions with centralized IT or archives functions;
- institutions in low-connectivity environments;
- institutions whose legal mandate restricts publication or data sharing;
- institutions choosing not to deploy AI.

Distinguish genuine absence of governance capability from legitimate context, centralized responsibility, or lawful non-use.

## 13. Analysis plan

The JDGF research lead should:

1. calculate item-level relevance summaries without treating them as proof of validity;
2. record all qualitative comments;
3. identify consensus, disagreement, and discipline-specific concerns;
4. maintain a decision log for every proposed change;
5. preserve rejected comments with reasons;
6. revise the framework before cognitive testing and institutional piloting;
7. publish an anonymized review summary unless reviewers consent to attribution.
