# JDGF Institutional Pilot Protocol

**Version:** 1.0 Candidate  
**Status:** Ready for authorized field use; no real pilot completed in this repository

## 1. Purpose

This protocol specifies how to conduct an institutional pilot of the Judicial Data Governance Framework (JDGF) and its assessment instrument. The pilot is designed to test feasibility, interpretability, evidence availability, scoring reliability, institutional usefulness, and safeguards before JDGF v1.0 is declared empirically validated.

## 2. Pilot objectives

The pilot shall determine whether:

1. JDGF dimensions and items are relevant to the participating institution;
2. respondents and assessors interpret the items consistently;
3. credible evidence can be obtained without disproportionate burden;
4. two independent assessors produce acceptably similar scores;
5. critical-control gates function as intended;
6. the resulting maturity profile supports useful and proportionate improvement planning;
7. confidentiality, judicial independence, rights, and institutional security are preserved.

## 3. Eligibility and authorization

A real pilot requires:

- written authorization from the competent institutional authority;
- a named institutional sponsor and assessment lead;
- agreement on scope, data domains, sites, participants, and assessment period;
- applicable ethics, research, data-protection, security, and records approvals;
- conflict-of-interest declarations;
- a secure evidence-handling plan;
- informed participation procedures where interviews or surveys involve individuals.

The framework team must not represent a simulated or desk-based exercise as an institutional pilot.

## 4. Recommended pilot sample

The initial multi-site validation should include varied institutional settings, where feasible:

- a central justice or judicial-administration body;
- at least one first-instance court;
- at least one appellate or higher court;
- a prosecution service or equivalent;
- a prison or corrections administration;
- a judicial statistics, archives, or digital-transformation function.

A single-institution pilot is useful for feasibility but cannot establish general applicability.

## 5. Participants

Participants should represent strategic, tactical, operational, technical, legal, records, statistics, security, privacy, audit, and user perspectives. Suggested roles include:

- executive sponsor;
- judicial or administrative leadership;
- data owners and stewards;
- registrars and court clerks;
- records and archives officers;
- statistics personnel;
- information-system and security personnel;
- privacy or legal advisers;
- internal audit or assurance;
- approved external assessors.

## 6. Scope definition

The pilot plan shall record:

- institutional entities and locations;
- judicial functions included;
- data domains and systems;
- assessment period;
- excluded activities and reasons;
- critical dimensions and critical items;
- intended outputs and users;
- legal and security restrictions.

## 7. Pilot design

### 7.1 Assessor structure

At least two trained assessors should independently score a representative subset covering all seven dimensions. They should not reconcile scores until independent scoring is complete.

### 7.2 Evidence collection

Use triangulation across:

- approved policies and mandates;
- role appointments and committee records;
- registers, catalogues, data dictionaries, and metadata;
- access, incident, quality, retention, and issue records;
- system-generated logs and reports;
- statistical production and publication procedures;
- interviews and workshops;
- direct observation and control walkthroughs;
- samples of critical judicial data and exchanges.

No sensitive case content should be copied unless strictly necessary, authorized, and protected.

### 7.3 Cognitive testing

After selected items, ask respondents and assessors:

- what they understood the item to mean;
- which evidence they considered relevant;
- which terms were ambiguous;
- whether the item fit their institutional context;
- whether response options distinguished real capability levels.

### 7.4 Burden measurement

Record:

- preparation time;
- interview and workshop duration;
- document-retrieval effort;
- assessor time by dimension;
- unavailable evidence;
- participant feedback on feasibility.

## 8. Data collection instruments

The pilot package shall include:

1. institutional authorization form;
2. scope and context form;
3. participant information and consent materials where required;
4. document request list;
5. JDGF Assessment Instrument;
6. evidence register;
7. independent scoring sheets;
8. disagreement and moderation log;
9. cognitive-interview guide;
10. burden and feasibility questionnaire;
11. findings validation record;
12. final pilot report template.

## 9. Analysis plan

### 9.1 Descriptive analysis

Report:

- item, attribute, dimension, and overall score distributions;
- missing and N/A rates;
- evidence-confidence ratings;
- critical findings and caps;
- time and resource burden;
- qualitative feedback and ambiguity themes.

### 9.2 Inter-rater reliability

For independently scored items and dimensions:

- calculate exact and adjacent agreement;
- use weighted kappa for ordinal item scores where assumptions permit;
- use an appropriate intraclass correlation coefficient for dimension scores;
- report confidence intervals;
- inspect systematic rater bias and high-disagreement items.

### 9.3 Content and response-process validity

Use structured expert ratings and cognitive findings to evaluate relevance, clarity, comprehensiveness, and contextual fit. Preserve an item-decision log showing retain, revise, split, merge, reclassify, or remove decisions.

### 9.4 Feasibility

Assess evidence availability, completion rates, burden, security constraints, and institutional usefulness. Items with persistent missingness require clarification, alternative evidence guidance, or reconsideration.

## 10. Provisional acceptance criteria

The pilot is considered sufficient to support a v1.0 release decision only if:

- every dimension has been tested in at least one real institutional setting;
- critical items have been cognitively tested and independently scored;
- at least 80% of applicable items can be supported by credible evidence;
- dimension-level agreement meets the provisional threshold in the Maturity Model or deviations are explained and corrected;
- no unresolved ambiguity materially affects rights, integrity, security, continuity, or scoring;
- participating institutions judge the resulting roadmap useful and proportionate;
- limitations and contextual boundaries are publicly documented without exposing sensitive information.

A broader claim of cross-institutional validity requires multiple heterogeneous pilots.

## 11. Ethics, confidentiality, and security

The pilot shall apply data minimization, need-to-know access, secure storage and transmission, controlled working papers, retention limits, and approved disposal. Findings must avoid exposing case details, protected identities, security weaknesses, or information that could compromise judicial independence or proceedings.

Participants must be able to correct factual errors. Institutional authorities may identify lawful confidentiality constraints but may not suppress supported scientific findings within the agreed reporting process.

## 12. Change control

Every change arising from the pilot shall record:

- affected requirement or item;
- evidence and rationale;
- impact on scoring and comparability;
- decision authority;
- version introduced;
- need for retesting.

Material scoring changes require a new validation round.

## 13. Pilot outputs

The pilot shall produce:

- anonymized institutional context;
- scope and method;
- evidence-availability profile;
- independent and moderated scores;
- reliability analysis;
- cognitive and feasibility findings;
- item revision log;
- institutional improvement roadmap;
- limitations and validation conclusion.

## 14. Current repository status

No authorized institutional dataset, participant record, evidence register, or independently scored case has been provided to this repository. Accordingly, the real institutional pilot remains **pending**. The protocol is complete enough to begin fieldwork once an institution formally authorizes participation.
